🎯 Guided exploration

  1. With the right-skewed population and n = 5, draw one sample. Which values appear in the sample plot? Where does the one sample mean appear?
  2. Add 1,000 samples. Compare the population histogram to the distribution of sample means. Are they identical?
  3. Change the sample size to n = 30, draw 1,000 samples, and observe the histogram. What happened to its shape and spread?
  4. Repeat with n = 100. Which is narrower: the distribution for n = 5 or n = 100? What happened to the center?
✅ Check what you should notice

Each sample produces one sample mean. With many repeated samples, the distribution of those means centers near the population mean. When n increases, sample means vary less from sample to sample (smaller standard error), and for sufficiently large n the sampling distribution is approximately Normal even when the original population is skewed.
